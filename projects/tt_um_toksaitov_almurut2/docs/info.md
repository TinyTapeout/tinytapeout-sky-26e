## How it works

Almurut II is a 32-bit teaching computer with its own instruction set and an AI accelerator, made to teach computer organization, compilers, and operating systems. Its CPU has thirteen 32-bit registers, a visible stack, explicit flags, interrupts and exceptions, a kernel mode and a user mode, and paging with two levels of page tables and eight remembered translations, over 4 GB of physical addresses. An instruction cache of 32 words, an interrupt controller, a timer, and a keyboard controller share the chip with it. The emulator, the assembler, the C compiler, the kernel, and the firmware of the bridge are in the platform repository, [asc2-project](https://github.com/toksaitov/asc2-project).

The AI accelerator runs the layers of small neural networks. It keeps twelve weights and twelve inputs of a byte each on the chip, in three rows, and four cells multiply signed bytes and add the products up. `dotv` adds all twelve products to a register, so a neuron with twelve inputs takes one instruction, and `dotm` gives the sum of each row to a register of its own, three neurons at once. `learn` corrects the weights by an error, a step of training on the chip, and `xsh` moves the inputs on by one byte, for a filter over a stream. `dot4` multiplies the four bytes of one register with those of another, `ssat` and `usat` hold a result within the range of a byte (`usat` is the rectified linear unit of a network), and `popcnt` counts bits, for networks of one bit a weight. The kernel of the platform uses `dot4` in its scheduler, as a small network that guesses whether a process will sleep before its turn ends.

A microcontroller beside the chip, the RP2350B of the Tiny Tapeout demo board, is its bridge to memory, storage, and various peripherals: it serves the RAM, the boot ROM, a console, a disk, a clock, a screen, and a button. They talk over the eight bidirectional pins, one byte at a time, with two strobes: the chip changes REQ for each byte it sends or takes, and the bridge answers each change with a change of ACK. A request is a header byte, four address bytes (a fetch of the next word goes without them), and the data of a write; the answer is a status byte and the data of a read. A sequence bit in every header lets the bridge see a request that comes twice. FRAME is high while a request is open, and UIO_OE is high while the chip drives the bus. The rules of the bus are in [bus-protocol-v1.md](https://github.com/toksaitov/asc2-project/blob/main/asc2-kb/bus-protocol-v1.md) of the platform. The free output `uo[4]` is always high, as a silent serial line is, and the three after it are always low.

Three more inputs make the CPU easy to watch and to teach with:

* Step-by-step execution: while STOP is high, the CPU waits between two instructions. Raising it and lowering it for one instruction runs the program step by step. With the instruction cache on, the debug port is read before each step: its restart empties the cache, and the instruction shows as a fetch on the bus.
* A debug port that shows all 643 bits of the CPU's state, with a clock input, a data input, and a data output. Each rise of the clock shows the next bit on the output, or bit 0 again if the data input is high. The bits are the instruction pointer, the instruction, the cause of the last trap, the flags, the control bits, the state of the CPU and of the bus, and every register. A kernel can lock the port: it then shows one bit that tells of the lock and zeros in every other place, until the next reset.
* A PS/2 keyboard: the chip receives its two lines, and the bridge can play a keyboard on them.

## How to test

The chip runs with the Almurut II firmware on the microcontroller of the demo board: the bridge in [asc2-bridge](https://github.com/toksaitov/asc2-project/tree/main/asc2-bridge) of the platform, built for the demo board and for the address of the design on the shuttle as [its Readme](https://github.com/toksaitov/asc2-project/blob/main/asc2-bridge/Readme.md) tells, and loaded with BOOT held while the USB cable goes in. The firmware selects the design, drives the clock (12.5 MHz) and the reset, holds the reset for at least 20 clocks, and then answers the requests of the chip. The first request is a fetch at address 0x00800000, the start of the boot ROM. The eight switches of the board are off, its three Pmod sockets are empty, and nothing is wired to its clock header: the bridge drives the same pins. The stock firmware of the board gives way to this one, since the chip takes its reset with the clock running, for 11 clocks at least, and reads the bridge's boot ROM from its first fetch.

The host script [bridge.py](https://github.com/toksaitov/asc2-project/blob/main/asc2-bridge/host/bridge.py) of the platform loads a program into the bridge and is its terminal. `bridge.py run asc2-firmware/hello` prints `Hello from Almurut II!` from the chip. With its disk loaded, the kernel [Rach2](https://github.com/toksaitov/asc2-project/tree/main/asc2-rach2) boots to a shell with its utilities and the showcase: `tour` there runs ten programs in about eight minutes, from the AI accelerator at work to 3D graphics and a short film on the screen, and `mark` measures the machine.

## External hardware

The demo board alone runs the whole computer: its microcontroller is the bridge, and a computer with a USB port is the terminal. An OLED screen of 128 by 64 pixels (SSD1306 or SH1106) and a push button on the board's analog header add graphics and games; the header comes without pins, and the [Readme of the bridge](https://github.com/toksaitov/asc2-project/blob/main/asc2-bridge/Readme.md) gives the wires.
